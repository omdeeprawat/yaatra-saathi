NANDA DEVI RAJ JAT YATRA — COMPLETE ROUTE GUIDE

Starting Point: Nauti Village
The Yatra begins at Nauti village in Chamoli district, Uttarakhand. Nauti is located approximately 20 km from Karnprayag. The initial assembly and rituals take place here, and the Chausingha ram formally joins the procession at Nauti. Altitude: 1,400 meters.

Stage 1: Nauti to Kulsari
Distance: 12 km. The first stage passes through the valley floor, offering views of the Pindar River. Kulsari is a small settlement with a significant temple dedicated to Nanda Devi. Altitude: 1,350 meters.

Stage 2: Kulsari to Nandkesari
Distance: 14 km. The route ascends gradually through oak and rhododendron forests. Nandkesari has one of the oldest Nanda Devi temples along the route. Altitude: 1,600 meters.

Stage 3: Nandkesari to Ramni
Distance: 15 km. Pilgrims cross several small streams. The forests become denser and wildlife including Himalayan birds can be spotted. Altitude: 2,100 meters.

Stage 4: Ramni to Narayanbagar
Distance: 10 km. The route descends briefly before climbing again. Narayanbagar is an important halt with better facilities. Altitude: 1,800 meters.

Stage 5: Narayanbagar to Mundoli
Distance: 18 km. A longer stage passing through cultivated terraced fields and traditional Garhwali villages. Altitude: 2,000 meters.

Stage 6: Mundoli to Wan
Distance: 14 km. Wan is one of the most important villages on the route. Grand rituals are performed here and the Yatra halts for two nights. Altitude: 2,400 meters.

Stage 7: Wan to Bedni Bugyal
Distance: 10 km. This is one of the most beautiful stages. Bedni Bugyal is a vast high-altitude meadow at 3,354 meters, often called one of the most beautiful bugyals (meadows) in the Himalayas. The Bedni Kund lake here is sacred to Nanda Devi. Panoramic views of Trishul, Nanda Ghunti, and other peaks.

Stage 8: Bedni Bugyal to Pathar Nachuni
Distance: 8 km. The terrain becomes more rugged and rocky. Altitude: 3,700 meters. Snow may be present even in summer.

Stage 9: Pathar Nachuni to Bagubasa
Distance: 5 km. The final camp before Homkund. Altitude: 4,000 meters. All pilgrims are advised to rest here and acclimatize. The night here is extremely cold.

Final Stage: Bagubasa to Homkund
Distance: 5 km. The sacred destination. Homkund is a glacial lake at 4,200 meters surrounded by snow-capped peaks. The Chausingha ram is ceremonially sent forward from here. This is the most emotionally charged part of the Yatra.

Total Distance: Approximately 280 km including all stages.
Total Duration: 19 to 22 days depending on weather and ritual halts.

Key Bugyals (Alpine Meadows) on the Route
Bedni Bugyal — 3,354 meters — most beautiful campsite
Ali Bugyal — 3,600 meters — expansive grassland with mountain views
Patar Nachuni — 3,700 meters — rocky meadow with glacier views

Important Rivers Crossed
Pindar River — crossed multiple times in the lower stages
Nandakini River — a significant tributary crossed mid-route
Several glacial streams in the upper reaches

Best Time to Observe Wildlife on the Route
The forests between Nauti and Wan are home to Himalayan monal pheasant, Himalayan black bear, musk deer, and various raptor species. Pilgrims are advised not to disturb wildlife.
