NANDA DEVI RAJ JAT YATRA — COMPLETE OVERVIEW

What is the Nanda Devi Raj Jat Yatra?
The Nanda Devi Raj Jat Yatra is the world's largest Himalayan pilgrimage, held once every twelve years in the Garhwal and Kumaon regions of Uttarakhand, India. It is considered the most sacred pilgrimage in the Kumaoni and Garhwali Hindu traditions. The Yatra is dedicated to Goddess Nanda Devi, who is worshipped as the daughter of the hills and a form of Goddess Parvati.

Historical Significance
The pilgrimage has been held for centuries, with historical records dating back over 1000 years. It is believed to re-enact the journey of Goddess Nanda returning to her husband Lord Shiva's abode on Mount Kailash. The Yatra is not merely a religious event but a living tradition that binds together the mountain communities of Uttarakhand, Kumaon, and Garhwal.

The Frequency
The Yatra is held once every twelve years according to the Hindu lunar calendar. Past Yatras were held in 1987, 2000, and 2014. The next Yatra is expected in 2026. The twelve-year cycle corresponds to the cycle of Jupiter around the sun, which holds deep astrological significance in Hindu tradition.

The Sacred Ram — Chausingha
The most distinctive element of the Yatra is the Chausingha, a four-horned ram (sheep). According to tradition, a naturally born four-horned ram is identified in the mountains and is considered the divine incarnation. This ram leads the procession and is ultimately set free at Homkund to proceed to the divine abode. Finding the Chausingha before the Yatra is itself considered a sacred sign.

Duration and Distance
The Yatra covers approximately 280 kilometers over 19 to 22 days. The route begins at Nauti village in Chamoli district and ends at Homkund, a sacred glacial lake at an altitude of approximately 4,200 meters above sea level. The journey traverses dense forests, alpine meadows, glacial valleys, and high-altitude terrain.

Number of Participants
In recent Yatras, over 100,000 pilgrims have participated. Local villagers from the mountain communities form the backbone of the procession, and pilgrims from across India and the world join them.
