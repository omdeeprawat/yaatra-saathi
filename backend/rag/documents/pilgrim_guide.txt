NANDA DEVI RAJ JAT YATRA — PRACTICAL PILGRIM GUIDE

Physical Fitness Requirements
The Yatra is a serious high-altitude trek covering 280 km over approximately 20 days. Pilgrims must be in good physical health. The route reaches altitudes of 4,200 meters which can cause altitude sickness. People with heart conditions, severe respiratory issues, or high blood pressure should consult a doctor before attempting the full route. Many pilgrims join for only part of the route. There is no compulsion to complete the entire journey.

Age Considerations
Children below 10 years are generally advised against the high-altitude stages above Wan. Senior pilgrims often participate in the lower stages and rejoin at accessible points. The stages from Nauti to Wan are manageable for most healthy adults of any age.

When to Start Preparing
Begin physical preparation at least 3 months before the Yatra. Regular walking, gradual hill climbing, and breathing exercises are recommended. Practice walking 10 to 15 km daily in the weeks leading up to departure.

Essential Items to Pack
Clothing: Warm thermal innerwear, fleece jacket, waterproof outer jacket, trekking pants, warm hat, gloves, wool socks. Temperatures drop below freezing above 3,500 meters even in summer months.
Footwear: Good quality waterproof trekking boots with ankle support. Never attempt the route in sandals or sneakers. Break in your boots before the Yatra.
Rain gear: A lightweight waterproof poncho or rain jacket. The Himalayan weather is unpredictable and monsoon rains are possible.
Walking pole or bamboo staff: Extremely helpful on steep and slippery terrain.
Sleeping: A sleeping bag rated to -5 degrees Celsius or lower for the upper camps.
Medical kit: Basic first aid including bandages, antiseptic, paracetamol, ibuprofen, oral rehydration salts, blister treatment, and diamox (for altitude sickness — consult your doctor).
Headlamp with extra batteries.
Water bottle and water purification tablets.
Sunscreen SPF 50 and UV-protection sunglasses (UV radiation is intense at altitude).
High-energy snacks: Nuts, dried fruits, energy bars, and chocolate.

Water and Hydration
Drink at least 3 to 4 liters of water per day, especially at higher altitudes. Natural spring water is available along the route but should be purified. Dehydration significantly worsens altitude sickness.

Food Along the Route
Basic food including dal, roti, rice, and tea is available at community kitchens (langars) and small stalls along the route, especially in villages. Above Wan, food availability becomes limited. Carry sufficient personal snacks for the high-altitude stages.

Altitude Sickness — Recognition and Response
Symptoms include headache, nausea, dizziness, fatigue, and difficulty breathing. If you experience any of these above 3,000 meters: rest immediately, drink water, and do not ascend further until symptoms improve. If symptoms are severe — including confusion, inability to walk straight, or bluish lips — descend immediately. This is a medical emergency. Never ignore altitude sickness symptoms.

Medical Facilities
A government medical team accompanies the Yatra procession. Basic medical camps are set up at major halts including Wan and Bedni Bugyal. For serious emergencies, helicopter evacuation is coordinated by the district administration.

Registration and Permissions
All pilgrims must register with the district administration. Registration is typically free or involves a nominal fee. A registration card must be carried throughout the journey. The district administration of Chamoli manages coordination.

Accommodation
Most halts involve open camping or basic accommodation in temporary shelters set up by the administration. Bring your own tent for the upper stages. In villages, some guesthouses and homestays are available.

Environmental Responsibility
The Yatra passes through ecologically sensitive Himalayan terrain including areas near the Nanda Devi Biosphere Reserve. Pilgrims are strictly asked not to litter, not to light fires in restricted zones, and not to disturb wildlife. Plastic is banned on the route. Carry your waste back.

Communication
Mobile network coverage exists in the valley areas up to Wan. Above Wan, there is no mobile network. Satellite phones are available with the administration medical team. Inform your family of your expected daily stages before losing network coverage.

Photography
Photography is welcomed throughout the Yatra. During ritual performances inside temples or during the doli procession, seek permission before photographing priests or the Goddess image directly. Many villagers are happy to be photographed but always ask first.

Language
Hindi is widely understood. The local languages are Garhwali and Kumaoni. Learning a few greetings in these languages — such as Jai Nanda (Glory to Nanda Devi) — is deeply appreciated by local communities.

Emergency Contacts
District Magistrate Chamoli: Contact via district administration office.
State Disaster Response Force: Available for mountain emergencies.
Always carry your registration card and emergency contact information on your person.
